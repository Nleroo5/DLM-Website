### Section 1: Impressions - Are People Seeing Your Ad? (250 words)

**What it is** (Simple definition):
- How many times your ad appeared on someone's screen
- Think of it like a billboard: impressions = how many cars drove past it

**Why it matters**:
- First checkpoint: If impressions are low, nobody's seeing your message
- Helps diagnose if your targeting is too narrow or budget too small

**What numbers are good**:
- Depends on your budget and audience size
- General benchmark: At least 1,000+ impressions per day for local businesses
- Red flag: Under 500 impressions per day means your ad isn't reaching enough people

**What to do**:
- Low impressions? Widen your targeting or increase budget
- High impressions but no results? Your ad creative or offer needs work

### Section 2: Clicks (Link Clicks) - Are People Interested? (300 words)

**What it is** (Simple definition):
- Number of people who clicked on your ad to learn more
- Shows genuine interest, not just passive scrolling

**Why it matters**:
- Separates the curious from the scrollers
- Indicates your ad creative is compelling enough to take action
- Directly affects your cost per lead

**What numbers are good**:
- Aim for 50-100+ clicks per week minimum for local campaigns
- Quality over quantity: 20 highly targeted clicks > 200 random clicks

**Click-Through Rate (CTR) explained**:
- CTR = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100
- Simple example: 100 clicks from 10,000 impressions = 1% CTR
- Good CTR for Facebook ads: 1-3% (varies by industry)
- Dental/medical: 1-2% is solid
- Restaurants/retail: 2-4% is achievable
- Professional services: 0.8-1.5% is typical

**What to do**:
- Low CTR? Test different images, headlines, or offers
- High CTR but no conversions? Your landing page needs improvement

**Practical example**:
"Sarah runs a dental practice in Atlanta. Her ad got 15,000 impressions and 300 clicks last week. That's a 2% CTR - solid for healthcare. But she's only getting 3 phone calls. The clicks are working; her next step is improving her website or offer."

### Section 3: Cost Per Click (CPC) - What Are You Paying? (250 words)

**What it is** (Simple definition):
- How much you pay each time someone clicks your ad
- Total ad spend ÷ number of clicks = CPC

**Why it matters**:
- Directly impacts your budget efficiency
- Shows if you're overpaying for traffic
- Helps calculate realistic budgets

**What numbers are good**:
- Atlanta market averages: $0.50 - $2.50 per click (varies by industry)
- Competitive industries (law, dental): $1.50 - $3.00
- Less competitive (retail, restaurants): $0.40 - $1.50
- Service businesses: $0.80 - $2.00

**What to do**:
- High CPC? Your targeting might be too competitive or ad relevance is low
- Improve your ad quality score with better creative and targeting
- Test different audience segments

**Real math example**:
"John spent $300 on ads and got 200 clicks. His CPC is $1.50. For his industry (home services), that's reasonable. If he was paying $4+ per click, something would be wrong with his targeting."

### Section 4: Cost Per Lead (CPL) - Are You Getting Customers? (350 words)

**What it is** (Simple definition):
- How much you pay for each potential customer contact (phone call, form submission, message)
- Total ad spend ÷ number of leads = CPL
- THE most important metric for most businesses

**Why it matters**:
- This is where rubber meets road
- You can have great clicks and traffic, but if people aren't contacting you, nothing else matters
- Determines if your ads are profitable

**What is a lead**:
- Phone call to your business
- Contact form submission
- Facebook/Instagram message request
- Appointment booking
- Email signup (for some businesses)

**What numbers are good** (Atlanta market):
- Dental practices: $30 - $80 per lead
- Home services (plumbing, HVAC): $25 - $60 per lead
- Restaurants: $5 - $15 per lead (for special offers/events)
- Professional services: $40 - $100 per lead
- Retail/e-commerce: Varies widely, $10 - $50

**Important context**:
- Your acceptable CPL depends on your profit margins
- Example: If you make $1,000 profit per customer, paying $50 per lead is great
- Example: If you make $100 profit per sale, paying $50 per lead probably won't work

**How to calculate YOUR acceptable CPL**:
1. Average customer value: $______
2. Typical close rate: _____% (how many leads become customers)
3. Profit margin: _____%
4. Your maximum CPL = (Customer Value × Close Rate × Profit Margin) ÷ 3

**Simple example**:
"Maria owns a salon. Average customer spends $200. She closes 30% of leads. She can afford to pay: $200 × 0.30 = $60 ÷ 3 = $20 per lead max. Her current ads are getting leads at $15 each. They're working!"

**What to do**:
- Track leads religiously (calls, forms, messages)
- If CPL is too high: Improve your targeting, offer, or landing page
- If CPL is good: Scale up your budget to get more leads

### Section 5: Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) - Are You Making Money? (300 words)

**What it is** (Simple definition):
- How much revenue you generate for every dollar spent on ads
- Total revenue from ads ÷ ad spend = ROAS
- Expressed as ratio (3:1) or multiple (3x)

**Why it matters**:
- The ultimate profitability metric
- Shows if your advertising is a good investment
- Helps you decide whether to increase or decrease budget

**What numbers are good**:
- Minimum viable: 2:1 (you make $2 for every $1 spent)
- Good performance: 3:1 to 5:1
- Excellent performance: 5:1 to 10:1
- Unicorn territory: 10:1+

**Reality check**:
- Different industries have different benchmarks
- E-commerce often needs 3-4:1 minimum to be profitable after costs
- Service businesses with high margins can profit at 2:1
- Don't compare your restaurant ROAS to someone's software business

**How to calculate YOUR ROAS**:
1. Track revenue from Facebook ads (use tracking, ask customers "how'd you hear about us?")
2. Add up monthly revenue from ad leads: $______
3. Divide by monthly ad spend: $______
4. That's your ROAS

**Simple example**:
"Tom spent $1,000 on Facebook ads last month. Those ads generated 15 customers who spent a total of $4,500. His ROAS is 4.5:1. For every dollar he spends, he makes $4.50 back. That's working."

**What to do**:
- ROAS below 2:1? Fix your offer, targeting, or sales process
- ROAS above 3:1? Consider increasing budget to scale
- Track properly: Use Meta Pixel, UTM parameters, or ask every lead

### Bonus Section: Putting It All Together (200 words)

**The dashboard you should create**:
- Weekly snapshot template
- Track all 5 metrics side by side
- Spot trends and problems quickly

**Example healthy ad campaign**:
```
Impressions: 25,000
Clicks: 500 (2% CTR)
CPC: $1.50
Total Spend: $750
Leads: 15 (CPL: $50)
Revenue: $3,000 (ROAS 4:1)
```

**Red flags to watch for**:
1. High impressions, low clicks = Bad creative or targeting
2. High clicks, low leads = Weak landing page or offer
3. Good leads, bad ROAS = Sales process or pricing issue

**The one metric you can't ignore**:
- Cost Per Lead is your North Star
- If CPL is good for your business, keep running ads
- If CPL is too high, diagnose using the other 4 metrics
