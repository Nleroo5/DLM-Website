### 12. Comprehensive FAQ Section (800 words)

#### FAQ 1: How much should I spend on Meta ads per month?
**Answer (120 words):**
The minimum effective budget for small businesses is $600/month ($20/day), which allows Meta's algorithm to exit the learning phase and optimize your campaigns. The average small business spends $427/month (Stat #2 reference), but we recommend starting at $30-50/day ($900-1,500/month) if you want to see meaningful results within 30-60 days. Your specific budget depends on your industry, profit margins, and customer lifetime value. For example, if your average sale is $100 with a 40% margin, you need roughly $15 cost per acquisition to break even (2.5:1 ROAS). Start with a budget that allows for 10-20 conversions per month minimum—this gives Meta enough data to optimize. Don't start smaller than $20/day, or you'll spend money without reaching the optimization phase.

#### FAQ 2: How long does it take to see results from Meta ads?
**Answer (110 words):**
Expect to wait 7-14 days before judging campaign performance. Here's the realistic timeline: Days 1-7 are the learning phase where Meta tests your ads across different audiences and placements. Results during this phase are unpredictable—don't panic if ROAS is low. Days 8-14 show initial stabilization as the algorithm identifies what works. Days 15-30 deliver consistent, predictable results. Most small businesses achieve their target ROAS (like 6:1) within 60-90 days after creative testing and optimization. The biggest mistake is changing campaigns too early—give each campaign at least 7 days and 50 conversion events before making decisions. Patience during setup pays off with sustained results later.

#### FAQ 3: What's better for small businesses: Advantage+ or manual campaigns?
**Answer (130 words):**
Advantage+ campaigns are better for 90% of small businesses in 2026. Meta's data shows Advantage+ Shopping campaigns deliver 22% higher ROAS and 9-32% lower cost per acquisition compared to manual campaigns (Stat #4 reference). Here's when to use each:

**Use Advantage+ if:** You're e-commerce selling products, you're generating leads for services, you have a budget above $30/day, or you want Meta's AI to handle targeting automatically.

**Use manual campaigns if:** You need highly specific targeting (e.g., B2B targeting companies with 50+ employees), you're running retargeting-only campaigns, or you need separate campaigns for different customer segments with different creatives.

For most small businesses starting out, launch an Advantage+ campaign first. You can always add manual campaigns later for specific use cases like retargeting.

#### FAQ 4: Do I really need a Meta Pixel, or can I run ads without it?
**Answer (120 words):**
You absolutely need the Meta Pixel installed and working correctly—it's non-negotiable for profitable campaigns in 2026. Without the Pixel, Meta can't track conversions (so you don't know if ads are working), optimize delivery (so it shows ads to random people, not likely buyers), or build retargeting audiences (so you lose 30-40% of potential revenue from repeat visitors). Installing the Pixel takes 15 minutes and is required for features like Advantage+ campaigns and Conversion API. Even if you don't make a sale in the first week, the Pixel collects data about visitor behavior (page views, add to cart, etc.) that Meta uses to find similar high-intent users. Don't skip this step—verify it's working with Meta Pixel Helper browser extension.

#### FAQ 5: What type of creative performs best in 2026?
**Answer (140 words):**
Short-form vertical video (9:16 format, 15-30 seconds long) dominates in 2026, particularly user-generated content (UGC) style that looks native to Facebook and Instagram feeds. Here's the winning formula:

1. **Hook:** Grab attention in first 3 seconds with a question, pattern interrupt, or bold statement
2. **Product in action:** Show your product/service being used, not just sitting pretty
3. **Native feel:** iPhone footage outperforms highly produced studio videos
4. **Captions:** 80% watch with sound off—add captions to every video
5. **Mobile-first:** Design for vertical mobile viewing (most users are on phones)

Static images still work but typically get 40-50% lower CTR than video. If you're not ready for video, use bright, high-contrast images showing results/transformation rather than just product shots. Test 3-5 variations and let data decide what works for YOUR audience.

#### FAQ 6: How do I know if my Meta ads are profitable?
**Answer (110 words):**
Calculate your break-even ROAS using this formula: **Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ profit margin**. For example, if you have a 40% profit margin (0.40), your break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5:1. Any ROAS above 2.5:1 means you're profitable. To find your current ROAS, check Meta Ads Manager: divide "Purchase Conversion Value" by "Amount Spent." If you see 6:1 ROAS with a 40% margin, you're making $1.40 profit for every $1 spent (after ad costs). Track ROAS over 7-14 day periods, not daily—fluctuations are normal. Also monitor cost per acquisition (CPA) and compare it to your customer lifetime value (LTV) for the full picture.

#### FAQ 7: What should I do if my ROAS is below 2:1?
**Answer (120 words):**
First, confirm you've given the campaign at least 7 days to exit the learning phase—premature optimization resets progress. If ROAS is still below 2:1 after 14 days, diagnose the problem systematically:

1. **Check Pixel:** Verify it's tracking conversions correctly (Meta Events Manager)
2. **Audit creative:** CTR below 0.8%? Your creative isn't engaging—test new videos/images
3. **Review landing page:** High CTR but low conversions? Your landing page is the problem, not the ad
4. **Analyze audience:** Check Ads Manager breakdown—are you reaching the right age/gender/location?
5. **Verify budget:** Below $30/day? You might not have enough data for optimization

Fix issues one at a time, wait another 7 days, then reassess. Most underperforming campaigns have creative or landing page problems, not targeting issues.

#### FAQ 8: Should I use Facebook, Instagram, or both?
**Answer (100 words):**
Use both—let Meta's algorithm determine which placement performs better through Advantage+ Placements (automatic placement optimization). In 2026, Meta automatically distributes your budget across Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, Stories, Reels, and other placements based on where you're most likely to get conversions. Don't manually select placements unless you have data showing one performs significantly worse. Most small businesses see about 60% of conversions from Facebook and 40% from Instagram, but this varies by audience and creative. Design creative that works for both platforms (vertical video, mobile-first) and let Meta optimize placement automatically for best results.
